\section{Introduction}

Graph neural networks remain attractive for spatial reasoning because they expose explicit topology, yet they also inherit a locality bias that makes long-range geometric reasoning difficult. This repository targets that gap through a multimodal setup that combines images, visible point-cloud graphs, and visibility-derived metrics.

The current implementation suggests a paper centered on two questions: first, whether shared latent spaces can align image observations with partial geometric graphs; second, whether those latents can support downstream localization against a larger scene graph. The resulting contribution would sit at the intersection of cross-modal representation learning, point-cloud graph modeling, and spatial reasoning.

Our working hypothesis is that visibility-aware preprocessing and graph-structured geometry provide a stronger inductive bias than image-only baselines for scene-level reasoning tasks such as reconstruction and localization.
